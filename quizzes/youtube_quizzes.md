## 00:50 — Q1

Must transmission vanish when E < V0?

A. Yes, because every electron is a classical ball.
B. No; a finite barrier can transmit probability current.
C. Only if the electron borrows energy.

Answer: B. A finite barrier has a wave solution with nonzero transmitted current. This is a probability prediction, not a classical trajectory.

## 02:24 — Q2

What is continuous at a finite step with constant mass?

A. Only the potential.
B. Only the wavefunction.
C. The wavefunction and its first derivative.
D. The second derivative must always match.

Answer: C. Integrating the Schrödinger equation across a finite step enforces continuity of psi and its first derivative for this constant-mass model.

## 04:15 — Q3

T = 0.1424 predicts what for 10,000 incident electrons?

A. Exactly 1,424 electrons in every trial.
B. An expected count near 1,424, with fluctuations.
C. Each electron loses 14.24% of its energy.

Answer: B. Transmission is a probability. For independent electrons the count is binomial, with standard deviation about 35; total energy is conserved.

## 06:02 — Q4

At fixed E = 0.5 eV and V0 = 1 eV, increasing a from 0.45 to 0.90 nm gives:

A. Half the original transmission.
B. About 0.00587 transmission.
C. About 0.2847 transmission.

Answer: B. The exact model gives T(0.45) = 0.142362 and T(0.90) = 0.005873. Width dependence is much stronger than a factor of two.

## 07:39 — Q5

Does a static lossless barrier require an energy loan?

A. Yes, for a short enough time.
B. No; total energy stays conserved.
C. Yes, if the transmitted wave is small.

Answer: B. The stationary solution has a fixed total energy throughout. Evanescent behavior does not require temporary energy nonconservation.

## 09:19 — Q6

Twice the STM current proves twice the surface height.

A. True.
B. False; bias, tip and electronic states also matter.
C. True, because tunneling is linear in distance.

Answer: B. Current is sensitive to gap and electronic structure. Control bias and tip condition and compare spectroscopy; a current ratio is not a height ratio.

## 10:48 — Q7

An exponential thickness trend alone conclusively proves direct tunneling.

A. Yes; no other process can produce that trend.
B. No; compare controls and competing mechanisms.
C. No; quantum mechanics forbids any current.

Answer: B. The trend supports tunneling but is not unique. Add temperature and bias series, defect controls, uncertainty and alternative-model comparisons.
